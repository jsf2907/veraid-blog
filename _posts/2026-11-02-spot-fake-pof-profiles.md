## Why do people catfish on dating sites?

Catfishing isn't one behavior with one motive. The fake profile you matched with last night could be a bored teenager, a lonely person hiding behind someone else's photos, or a professional scammer working a script.

### The four common motives, from boredom to money

Most catfish fall into four groups. Some want attention or an escape from their own life, using borrowed photos to live out a fantasy. Some are testing boundaries or trolling for reactions. Others run romance scams, building emotional trust over weeks before asking for money, gift cards, or crypto. The last group is the most expensive: the FTC reports that romance scams consistently rank among the costliest fraud categories, with reported losses in the hundreds of millions of dollars every year.

### Why travelers are targeted more than locals

Travelers make better targets. You're new to the area, your social circle is thin, and you're actively looking to meet people. A match who offers to show you around feels like a gift, not a risk. Scammers know this and time their approaches accordingly.

## What are the warning signs of a catfish profile on Plenty of Fish?

### Photo signals: one photo, studio quality, no candid shots

A profile built on a single photo is the oldest catfish tell on Plenty of Fish, and it still works because it costs nothing to copy. Studio-quality headshots are the second layer of the same signal: real people's photo libraries are messy, full of bad lighting, group shots, and pictures taken by friends. A match whose every image looks like a modelling portfolio is showing you a borrowed life.

### Bio signals: vague location, generic interests, fast emotional escalation

The bio fills in what the photos start. Watch for a location that stays vague or shifts between messages, interests so generic they could describe anyone (travelling, music, gym), and a story that never quite adds up.

The strongest single signal is pace. A real match in Colombia who wants to meet you will ask practical questions: where you're staying, what neighbourhood, when you're free. A catfish skips that logistics stage and moves straight to emotional intensity, telling you within a day or two that they've never felt this connection, or that they're falling for you before you've video-called. That escalation is deliberate. It builds attachment before you've had a chance to test anything, so that when you finally suggest verifying who they are before you meet, you feel like the one being difficult. A match is not a verification, and someone who resents a simple check-in request has told you exactly what you needed to know.

### Reverse image search and video-call tests that cost nothing

Save the profile photo and run it through Google Images or TinEye. A stock photo or a photo tied to a different name ends the conversation. Then ask for a short video call. A catfish almost always has a reason the camera "isn't working"; a real person just says hello.

### When voluntary verification is the tiebreaker

POF supports voluntary photo verification, and its own help pages explain how the process and reporting work. A verified profile is not a character reference, but when two profiles look equal, the verified one is the lower-risk conversation to open.

## How does POF's verification compare to other dating apps?

### What POF verifies and what it leaves open

POF offers photo verification, which asks you to submit a selfie matching a posed gesture so your profile can carry a verified marker. That covers one thing: the photos belong to a real person who took them recently. It does not cover name, age accuracy beyond self-reported fields, or whether the person behind the account is who they claim to be offline. Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge each run some version of photo or video selfie verification too, and none of them verify identity in the deeper sense. A verified badge on any of these apps means the profile passed a selfie check, not that the account holder's stated name, nationality, or job is accurate. If you are screening matches before traveling to Colombia, treat every badge the same way: a weak signal, not a conclusion.

### A match is not a verification: why badges are not enough

Badges confirm a face, not an intention. A catfish can pass selfie verification with their own face and still misrepresent everything else, or borrow a verified account entirely. ## When does catfishing cross into a scam you should report?

A fake profile is annoying. A scam is when the person behind it starts moving money out of your pocket. So watch for escalation, not just fakery.

### Money requests, off-platform moves, and gift cards

Three signals mark the line. First, any money request: an emergency, a customs fee, a plane ticket. Second, a push to leave POF for WhatsApp or Telegram before you've verified anything, which removes the platform's reporting tools. Third, gift cards, crypto, or wire transfers, which are the standard payout rails of romance fraud and nearly impossible to reverse.

### Reporting a profile on POF step by step

Open the conversation, tap the flag or report icon, choose the reason (scam or fake profile), and add specifics: the money request, the off-platform ask, any links they sent. Then block. If you already sent money, contact your bank immediately and file a report with the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). Reporting protects the next traveler, not just you.

## What should you do if you match with a catfish on POF?

Stop messaging first. Don't confront them or ask for explanations; confrontation just gives a catfish time to change accounts and tactics. Screenshot the profile, photos, and any messages that show deception, then use POF's report and block tools so the profile is flagged before it reaches anyone else.

### Cutting contact and reporting

Report through the app, then block. If money changed hands or the person claimed to be a tour guide or driver, keep your screenshots; they matter if you dispute a payment with your bank or card issuer. Don't re-engage to "get answers." A verified identity can't be faked retroactively, and neither can an apology.

### If you already shared photos, money, or personal details

Contact your bank immediately about any transfers, and change passwords if you reused one. If intimate photos were shared, stop contact and report through the platform; don't pay anyone threatening to release them, because paying rarely ends the demands. Watch your accounts for unusual activity over the following weeks.

## FAQ

### Can you get catfished on Plenty of Fish even if a profile has a verified badge?

Yes. POF's photo verification only confirms the selfie matches the photos on the profile. It says nothing about the name, age, job, or intentions behind the account. A catfish can pass the selfie check with their own face and still misrepresent everything else, or operate a borrowed, already-verified account.

### Is Plenty of Fish worse for catfishing than other dating apps?

There's no solid evidence it is. Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge all run some version of photo or selfie verification, and none of them verify identity in a deeper sense either. On any platform, a badge means the profile passed a face check, not that the person is honest. Apply the same screening checklist everywhere.

### Should I send my ID to someone I matched with to prove I'm real?

No. Sending a photo of your government ID to a stranger hands them material for identity theft and gives you nothing in return. If you want verification, use a service built for it, like VeraID, where your ID is checked through the platform and the other person only sees your photo, first name and verification status, never your document.

### How do I reverse image search a dating profile photo for free?

Save the photo to your device, then upload it to Google Images or TinEye, both free. If the image appears on a stock photo site, a modelling portfolio under a different name, or multiple profiles with different names, stop the conversation. It takes under two minutes and catches the laziest catfish immediately.

### What information should I never share with a POF match before meeting?

Never send money, gift cards, or crypto, and never share financial details, passwords, or copies of your ID. Hold back your home address, hotel name, and daily schedule until you've verified them through a video call. Also avoid intimate photos: if a scammer gets them, paying a blackmail demand rarely ends the threats.
