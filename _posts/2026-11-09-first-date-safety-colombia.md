First date safety when traveling means verifying who your match actually is before you fly to meet them, picking a public venue you control, and keeping your own transport and accommodation independent. A match is not a verification, and abroad you cannot easily reschedule, so the checks that matter most happen before you book anything.

## What Does First Date Safety Actually Mean When You Met Online?

When a friend introduces you, someone in your circle has vouched for that person. When you met online, nobody has. First date safety means closing that gap yourself: confirming the person is who they claim to be, choosing a public venue you control the exit from, and telling someone where you are and when to check in.

It also means watching for the most common online-meeting risk: financial manipulation. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center warns that romance scammers build trust quickly, then ask for money, often claiming an emergency ([IC3 warning on online dating fraud](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2019/PSA190805)). A request for funds before you have ever met in person is a stop signal, not a loyalty test.

## The Core First Date Safety Tips That Still Apply Everywhere

The baseline holds whether you're meeting a match in Bogotá or Boston: choose a public venue you picked or know, arrange your own transport both ways, tell a friend where you're going and when you expect to be back, and limit alcohol so your judgment stays intact.

### First date safety tips for women traveling alone

Meet in a busy café, restaurant, or bar rather than a private address or a car. Share your live itinerary with someone you trust at home, including the venue name and your expected return time, and check in with them by text mid-date. If your driver or the venue feels off, leave early; your own transport means you can. Keep your drink with you or in sight at all times.

### What to carry (and what to leave at your accommodation)

Carry a copy or photo of your passport rather than the original, plus one payment card and small cash. Leave spare cards, your passport, and extra valuables in your accommodation's safe. Charge your phone fully and carry a small power bank; a dead phone cuts off your maps, your ride, and your check-in with a friend in one go.

## A Match Is Not a Verification: Checking Who They Are Before You Meet

A dating profile is self-reported. Photos can be lifted, names can be borrowed, and a profile alone confirms nothing. The [FBI's romance scams explainer](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-crimes/romance-scams) notes that scammers use fake identities to build trust before asking for money. When impersonation crosses into fraud or extortion, it becomes a criminal matter, not just bad manners.

### Video calls: the cheapest verification you already have

Before paying for anything, ask for a short video call. A live face moving against your questions is harder to fake than a gallery of photos, and it costs nothing. If someone dodges every call with timezone excuses, treat that as data.

## Is It Illegal to Fake Your Identity Online?

Short answer: sometimes, and the line is thinner than most people think. Using a nickname or fake name on a dating profile is not a crime by itself in many places. It can become fraud when someone uses that false identity to get money, goods, or something of value from you, and impersonation laws can apply when someone pretends to be a specific real person. Rules vary by country, so treat this as general information, not legal advice. Romance scams sit squarely on the wrong side of that line.

But legality is the wrong question for your safety. Something can be perfectly legal and still be a reason to walk away: a 10-year-old photo, a borrowed profession, a first name that isn't theirs. Whether they'd be convicted matters less than whether the person at the table matches the identity you checked beforehand. If they dodged every check, that gap is your answer.

## Meeting Someone Online From a Different Country: What Changes

### Verifying identity across time zones before you fly

A check means less if you did it three weeks before your trip. Check again close to departure: ask for a fresh photo holding a dated note, confirm their phone number still works, do one live video call from their actual neighborhood, not a rehearsed room, and if they have a TRU ID, look it up again shortly before you meet. Time zones matter here. If someone in Colombia can only ever call between 9 p.m. and midnight Bogotá time, ask why. Shift work is a real answer; a second life is also a real answer.

### Red flags for international matches

A common pattern: the match pushes hard to move off the app, avoids video calls, and has a story ready for why they can't meet in a public place. Other repeated signs: they claim to be in your destination city but their photos are clearly from elsewhere, they ask you to bring something specific, and they get defensive when you suggest meeting somewhere you chose. None of these alone prove bad intent. A cluster of them means you should slow down.

### Money, gifts, and "customs fee" requests

If a match asks you to carry a package through the airport, pay a fee to release a package, or bring cash for a "customs" problem, end the contact. That request is a known setup, not a favor between new friends.

## Meeting People Abroad in Colombia: Local Logistics That Matter

### Getting there and back: transport choices

Arrange your own transport both ways so you control the arrival and the exit. In Bogotá, Medellín, and Cartagena, riders use apps such as DiDi, Cabify, and Uber; payment options vary, so confirm how you'll pay before you get in. Pick a venue in a busy, walkable area you already know from your first days in the city, not somewhere a match suggests across town. Daytime or early evening first meetings make an early exit easy.

### Staying reachable without a local number

Buy a prepaid SIM at the airport or a Claro or Movistar shop, or activate an eSIM before your flight, so you keep data and a working number all day. Save your accommodation address in Spanish in your notes app for drivers. Text a friend or family member back home the venue, the match's profile name, and your expected return time, then check in when you're back. If plans change mid-date, update them rather than leaving a stale plan.

## If the Date Goes Wrong: Exit Plans and Reporting

Set an exit before you arrive: a friend who expects a check-in text at a set time, a code word that means "call me with an excuse," and enough cash for a ride you book yourself, not one your date arranges. Leaving early is fine. You owe no explanation beyond "I've got to go."

If something happens, move first and document second. Get to a populated place like a mall or pharmacy, then call 123, use your phone's emergency feature, or ask staff for help.

For reporting in Colombia, you can file a report at a police CAI station or online through the Policía Nacional's [ADenunciar](https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/una-herramienta-al-servicio-ciudadania-adenunciar) system, and use the dating app's in-app reporting so the account gets flagged. If you looked up your date's TRU ID in VeraID, note it in your report along with the name and photo you saw, which makes the report more concrete than a screenshot of a profile.

## FAQ

### Can you get in trouble for pretending to be someone else on a dating app?

A fake name on a profile is not a crime by itself in many places. It can cross into fraud when someone uses that false identity to extract money, goods, or something of value, and impersonation laws can apply when they pose as a specific real person. Rules vary by country. Romance scams sit on the wrong side of that line, but legality is the wrong test for your safety.

### How do I verify someone I met online lives in another country before I visit?

Check again close to departure, not weeks ahead. Ask for a fresh photo holding a dated note, confirm their phone number still works, and do a live video call from their actual neighborhood rather than a rehearsed room. Watch the timezone pattern too: someone who can only ever call within a narrow late-night window deserves a direct question about why.

### What are warning signs when meeting an online match abroad?

Pushing hard to move off the app, dodging video calls, and having a ready excuse for avoiding a public meeting. Photos that clearly aren't from their claimed city, requests to bring something specific, and defensiveness about your chosen venue also recur. One sign proves nothing; a cluster means slow down.
