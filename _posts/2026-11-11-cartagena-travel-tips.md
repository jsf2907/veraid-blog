The best Cartagena travel tips start with one decision: when you go. December brings festivals and peak prices, September brings rain and empty plazas, and cruise days change how the old city feels by the hour. Pick your month first, then layer on airport, packing, neighborhood, and safety tips that match the season you're walking into.

## When is the best time of year to visit Cartagena?

The best months are December through April, when rainfall drops, humidity feels manageable, and the old city is at its liveliest. The trade-off: higher hotel prices and heavier crowds around holidays and cruise-ship days. If you want the same weather for less money, aim for the shoulder months.

### Dry season vs wet season

Cartagena's dry season runs roughly December to April; the wettest stretch is usually September to November, with short, heavy afternoon downpours rather than all-day rain. Temperatures hover around 30°C year-round, so the season changes rainfall, not heat.

### Shoulder months that hit the sweet spot

Late January, February, and early March give you dry weather after the holiday price spike. Late April into early May sits before the heavy rains and after the Easter crowds thin out. Both windows let you book better accommodations and enjoy the old city without peak-season volume.

## What happens in Cartagena each month of the year?

Cartagena has two real seasons: hot and dry (December to April) and hot and wet (May to November). Prices and crowds swing far more than the weather does, so the month you pick matters more than the forecast.

### January to April

This is the dry season: less rain, steady sea breezes, and the busiest, most expensive stretch of the year. It is also cruise season, so the old city gets crowded on days when ships dock. Late January usually brings the Hay Festival Cartagena, a literature and ideas festival ([programme and tickets](https://hayfestival.com/cartagena/tickets)). Semana Santa (Easter week, March or April) is a major Colombian holiday, and domestic travel pushes hotel rates up sharply. Book accommodation weeks ahead if you travel in this window.

### May to August

The rains start in May, usually as short afternoon downpours rather than all-day washouts, and prices drop with them. June and July bring Colombia's mid-year school holidays and some European summer traffic, so it's a middle ground: greener, cheaper than high season, but not empty. August is quieter and one of the better value months.

### September to December

September and October are the wettest and cheapest months, with the fewest visitors. November is a shoulder month, and the city's biggest party, the Cartagena Independence festivities, runs around November 11 with parades, music, and packed plazas. December flips to high season almost overnight: Colombian holiday travel from mid-December makes it busy and pricey through early January.

## How do you get from the airport to the old city without overpaying?

Rafael Núñez airport sits about 15 minutes from Centro and Getsemaní. Official taxi fares are set by the city, and the airport kiosk prints the right fare for your destination (around COP 16,000 to the old city in 2024, according to [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/getting-around-cartagena); fares rise most years). A driver asking for far more than the printed amount is giving you tourist pricing.

### Official fare slip vs ride apps

Get the fare slip at the kiosk just outside customs, show it to the driver at the taxi stand, and pay that amount when you arrive. This locks the price and removes negotiation. Ride apps such as Uber, DiDi and Cabify also operate in Cartagena and can be a good option, but the official taxi line is often faster when lots of flights or ships arrive at once.

### When to arrive to skip the queue

Cruise days swell arrivals and taxi lines. Check a port schedule such as [CruiseMapper's Cartagena calendar](https://www.cruisemapper.com/ports/cartagena-colombia-port-3733) before booking flights, and avoid landing when several ships are in port if you can.

## What should you wear and pack for Cartagena's heat and humidity?

Pack for 30°C+ days and heavy humidity year-round: lightweight linen or cotton, a wide-brimmed hat, polarized sunglasses, and reef-safe SPF 50. Two or three quick-dry shirts beat a full suitcase, since you can rinse them in the sink overnight.

### Dressing for the old city's dress codes

Some restaurants and rooftop bars in the walled city keep a smart-casual dress code in the evening, which can rule out shorts, sleeveless shirts, or flip-flops. One collared shirt and one pair of light trousers cover dinner reservations without adding weight. Churches expect covered shoulders and knees, so a scarf or light wrap doubles as sun protection by day and cover-up by night.

### Rainy-season additions

October and November bring short, intense downpours. Add a compact umbrella, a ziplock bag for your phone, and one pair of sandals you don't mind getting soaked. Skip rain jackets; they trap heat and dry slowly in the humidity.

## Which neighborhoods should you stay in and why?

### Centro and San Diego

Centro puts you inside the walled city: balconied colonial streets, plazas, and restaurants at your door, with the highest nightly rates and the most street noise near Plaza Santo Domingo. San Diego, in the northern part of the walled city, is quieter and slightly cheaper while keeping the same walkability. Both let you walk to dinner without hailing a ride after dark.

### Getsemaní

Just outside the walls from Centro, Getsemaní trades polish for street art, cheaper hostels and guesthouses, and a lively plaza scene around Plaza de la Trinidad. It is a short walk to the old city, but noise runs late, so light sleepers should book rooms off the main squares.

### Bocagrande

Bocagrande is the beach strip: high-rises, wider rooms, and lower prices per square meter, but you will taxi or bus into the old city for most sightseeing. If you travel during a festival, book early, since Centro rooms sell out first.

## How do you avoid common scams and overcharging in Cartagena?

### Taxi and menu overcharging

Most overcharging in Cartagena comes down to one thing: no agreed price. Taxis have no meters, so settle the fare before you get in, or use a ride app that shows the price up front. In restaurants, especially around the old city, check that menu prices include tax and service, and confirm the price of anything priced "by weight," like seafood. Count your change every time.

### Meeting people from apps and social media safely

A match is not a verification. If you meet someone through a dating app, a tour group, or a driver recommendation, remember that a profile photo and a name tell you little about who shows up. The habits that help: meet in public places first, tell someone at your hotel where you are going, and keep your own transport separate so you are never dependent on the other person for a ride. Save Colombia's emergency number, 123, in your phone.

## What day trips from Cartagena are actually worth it?

Three trips justify leaving the old city: the Rosario Islands, Barú, and Totumo. Timing matters more than the brochure you read, because the same boat trip can feel like paradise or a parking lot depending on when cruise ships dock.

### Rosario Islands

Book a speedboat or catamaran to Isla Grande or another island on a weekday, when boats and beaches are less crowded. Crossings can get bumpy on windy or stormy days, so ask the operator about sea conditions and life jackets. Confirm whether your lunch, chair, and marine park fee are included before paying.

### Playa Blanca and Barú

Playa Blanca is free but crowded by mid-morning. Stay overnight on Barú or leave the city early to get the white sand before the tour boats arrive.

### Totumo Mud Volcano

A half-day trip where you climb into a warm mud crater. Go early morning; the tiny changing facilities get chaotic once group tours stack up.

## How do you eat and drink well on a budget in Cartagena?

### Where locals eat cheap

Skip the walled city's plazas and head to Getsemaní or Pie de la Popa for lunch. Look for a corrientazo or menú del día, the midday set lunch: usually soup, rice, meat, plantain and a juice for a fixed price, often posted on a sign outside, and it runs a few dollars. If a menu in the old city has no prices printed, ask before you sit.

### Drinks and street food

Ceviche carts around Getsemaní sell generous portions for a fraction of restaurant prices; busy carts with locals in line are the safe bet. Fresh juice and agua de coco cost pocket change from street vendors. Aguardiente and Club Colombia beer are cheap almost everywhere, but check bar prices in the old city first, where a beer can cost several times the neighborhood rate.

## FAQ

### Is Cartagena safe for solo female travelers?

Many solo women visit Cartagena without problems, using standard precautions. Stick to Centro, San Diego, or Getsemaní after dark, use ride apps or official taxis at night, and agree on fares before entering any cab. Expect some street catcalling. Tell your accommodation your plans, keep valuables out of sight, avoid empty beaches like Playa Blanca after tour boats leave, and save 123 for emergencies.

### How many days do you need in Cartagena?

Three to four days covers the old city, Getsemaní, and a day trip to the Rosario Islands or Barú. Add a fifth day if you want the Totumo Mud Volcano or a slow morning in Bocagrande. The 30°C+ heat limits how much you can comfortably walk each day, so fewer packed days beat one exhausting sightseeing marathon.

### Do I need to speak Spanish to get around Cartagena?

Not for the basics: many hotels, restaurants, and tour operators in the tourist zones handle English. But prices drop and friction disappears with a few phrases, especially outside the walled city, where set-lunch kitchens and taxi drivers may speak little English. Learn numbers for fare negotiation, and carry a translation app for menus without printed prices.

### Is it safe to use dating apps in Cartagena as a tourist?

They are widely used, but a profile tells you little about who shows up. Meet first in public places like a busy plaza or café, tell someone at your hotel where you are going, and arrange your own transport so you are never dependent on your date for a ride. If the person you're meeting has a TRU ID, looking it up in VeraID adds one layer; it is not a substitute for the rest of your plan.

### How much cash should I carry in Cartagena?

Carry small amounts: around COP 100,000 to 200,000 in small bills covers ceviche carts, street vendors, juice, and short taxi rides. Cards work in most restaurants and hotels, but check whether menu prices include tax and service. Use ATMs inside banks or malls rather than street machines, and count your change every time.
