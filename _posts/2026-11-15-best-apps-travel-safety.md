## What are the best apps for travel safety?

The best app depends on your use case. Travel safety apps fall into four broad types:

- **SOS and monitoring apps:** a panic button that alerts contacts or a monitoring team and shares your location when triggered.
- **Family location sharing apps:** location check-ins and arrival alerts across a group.
- **Incident alert apps:** real-time alerts about incidents reported near you.
- **Identity lookup:** confirming who a person is before you meet them. That is where VeraID fits: guides, drivers, hosts and professionals verify their government ID with a biometric check plus human review and get a permanent TRU ID, and you look it up to see their photo, first name and verification status.

Your phone also has a built-in emergency SOS shortcut. Set it up before you travel, whatever else you install.

## What features matter most in a travel safety app?

Four criteria run through every recommendation in this guide: offline access, emergency SOS, location sharing, and identity verification. Most apps cover one or two well. Fewer cover all four, so the right pick depends on which job you're hiring it for.

### Why identity verification is a separate job from emergency response

Emergency SOS and location sharing help you after something has gone wrong. Identity verification helps you before you meet: it confirms that the tour guide, driver or host is who they say they are. SOS and family apps focus on response and awareness, while VeraID handles the verify-before-you-meet step, with visitor plans at $5.99, $14.99 and $19.99 USD. Many travelers use one of each.

### Why offline access matters more in Colombia than at home

Rural routes to Tayrona, Caño Cristales, or the Coffee Region can leave you without reliable data. An app whose SOS or check-in features die with the signal is a fair-weather tool. Test any app's offline behavior before you leave the city, not during the trip.

## Which travel safety apps work fully offline?

No consumer safety app is fully offline, because SOS alerts and check-ins need a connection to reach anyone. What differs is how much each tool keeps working when your data does not. Some location sharing apps show your last known location and catch up once you reconnect; panic buttons need a connection at the moment you press them. Practical setup for Colombia: download offline maps, buy a local eSIM or SIM before relying on any app, and treat offline mode as a buffer for reconnecting, not a substitute for a signal.

## Do travel safety apps drain battery or compromise privacy?

Battery drain comes mostly from continuous location features. An app that shares your location in the background all day costs more battery than an app you open only to look someone up before a meeting. If battery matters, pick apps that run check-ins on demand rather than in the background, and carry a power bank.

### Sharing your ID with an app: what to ask first

Be wary of any platform that demands an ID scan without saying why. With VeraID, visitors never upload their own ID: only the guides, drivers, hosts and professionals who want a TRU ID verify. ## How do travel safety apps integrate with local emergency services in Colombia?

Colombia's emergency number is 123, which covers police, fire, and medical response nationwide. Dial it directly rather than relying on an app's SOS button as your first move.

Many app SOS services are built around US emergency infrastructure and may not be able to send a Colombian ambulance or police patrol on your behalf. Treat their alerts as a way to notify contacts back home, not as a replacement for calling 123 yourself.

A check-in routine helps here: if you share your itinerary with a trusted contact before a tour or a driver pickup, someone already knows where you should be if you go quiet.
