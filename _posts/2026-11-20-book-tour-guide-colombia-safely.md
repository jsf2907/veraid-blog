- In Colombia, tour guides are tourism service providers that must be listed in the Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT), and guides need a professional guide card (tarjeta profesional).

To book a tour guide in Colombia safely, check three things before you pay: that the guide or agency has an RNT number, that the guide holds a professional guide card, and that the payment method and terms are ones you can trace and dispute. Then, on tour day, confirm the person who meets you is the person you booked. Most problems with guides start with a deposit sent to someone whose identity was never checked.

## Do tour guides in Colombia need a license?

Yes. Colombia's tourism law requires every tourism service provider to register in the Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT) before operating, and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism (MinCIT) lists tour guides ("los guías de turismo") among the providers that must register ([MinCIT, what is the RNT](https://www.mincit.gov.co/minturismo/analisis-sectorial-y-promocion/registro-nacional-de-turismo/que-es-el-registro-nacional-de-turismo)). The RNT is renewed every year between January 1 and March 31.

Guides also need a tarjeta profesional de guía de turismo. Colombia's Constitutional Court upheld that requirement in 2019, treating guiding as a profession that calls for proven training ([El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/turismo/los-guias-de-turismo-deben-tener-tarjeta-profesional-corte-constitucional-article-833280/)). Since 2020 the card has been issued online and free, and chambers of commerce check that a guide holds it before registering them in the RNT ([MinCIT, July 2020](https://www.mincit.gov.co/prensa/noticias/turismo/tarjeta-profesional-guia-de-turismo-en-tres-dias)).

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules can change, so treat the RNT and the guide card as the baseline to ask about, not a full legal checklist.

## How do I check a tour guide's RNT number?

Start by asking for it. MinCIT's legal guide for travel agencies says registered agencies must include their RNT number in all advertising, and operating agencies must use guides who are registered in the RNT ([MinCIT legal guide for travel agencies](https://www.mincit.gov.co/minturismo/analisis-sectorial-y-promocion/guias-legales-de-los-prestadores-de-servicios-turi/agencias-de-viajes-y-oficinas-de-representacio-1.aspx)). A listing, website or WhatsApp profile with no RNT number is not proof of a scam, but it is a reason to ask.

A few simple checks help:

- **Ask for the RNT number in writing.** A legitimate guide or agency will share it without fuss.
- **Ask to see the guide card.** A licensed guide can show their tarjeta profesional.
- **Match the names.** The name on the RNT, the guide card, the booking and the payment details should line up. A different name on the bank transfer is a red flag.
- **Search the business name** with words like "scam", "review" or "complaint", which is the FTC's own tip for checking travel companies ([FTC, avoid scams when you travel](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoid-scams-when-you-travel)).

## Where should I find a tour guide in Colombia?

The safest bookings come from sources that leave a trail: a registered agency, your hotel or hostel front desk, a well-known booking platform with its own payment and refund rules, or a guide recommended by someone you trust. The FTC suggests getting recommendations from family, friends or other trusted sources before you book ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoid-scams-when-you-travel)).

Be more careful with guides you meet through social media groups, comments or unsolicited messages. Many are honest independent guides, but these channels make it easy to copy someone else's photos, reviews and name. Before you send money, run the checks above and confirm the person is who they say they are.

## What are the red flags of a fake tour guide or tour operator?

Fake listings tend to share a few patterns. Watch for these before you pay:

- **Pressure to pay now.** "Only one spot left, pay in the next hour" is a classic push. The FTC advises refusing anyone who rushes you.
- **One payment method only.** If someone insists you can only pay by wire transfer, gift card or cryptocurrency, the FTC calls it "a sure sign of a scam", and says the same about demands to pay only with a payment app.
- **Vague details.** No exact meeting point, no itinerary, no list of what is included.
- **No cancellation policy.** The FTC's advice is to get the cancellation and refund policy before paying, and to walk away if you can't.
- **A price far below everyone else's.** Unusually cheap tours can be bait for a deposit.
- **Names that don't match.** The profile says one name, the payment goes to another.
- **No RNT number and a vague answer when you ask for it.**

## How should I pay a tour guide or agency in Colombia?

Pay in a way you can trace. A booking platform with its own refund rules, or a card payment to a registered business, leaves a record you can point to if something goes wrong. Card dispute rights depend on your bank and have deadlines, so keep your receipts and messages.

If a guide asks for a deposit, keep it small, get a written confirmation that shows the name, date, price and what the deposit covers, and pay the balance in person on the day. Don't send money to a third party's account "for the office", and don't change payment details because of a last-minute message.

## What if a different guide shows up?

Agencies sometimes send a different guide than the one you chatted with, for honest reasons like illness. The safe habit is simple: the agency should tell you in advance, with the new guide's name and, ideally, a photo or a way to check them. If someone unexpected appears at the meeting point and can't explain who sent them, don't go anywhere private with them. Call the agency on the number you already have, not a number the stranger gives you.

Before any tour, share your plans with a friend: the guide's name, the meeting point and your expected return time. For the latest official safety information, check the [U.S. State Department travel advisory for Colombia](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/colombia.html) for the current level before your trip.

## What can I do if I get scammed by a fake tour operator?

Act fast and keep everything: screenshots of the listing, chats, receipts and the account details you paid. Then:

- Contact your bank or card issuer right away about the payment.
- Report the listing to the platform where you found it.
- File a police report. In Colombia you can report online through the Policía Nacional's ADenunciar service ([Policía Nacional](https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/una-herramienta-al-servicio-ciudadania-adenunciar)).
- Complaints about tourism providers can go to Colombia's consumer authority, the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC), which MinCIT's legal guide lists for complaints against tourism providers.
- US travelers can also report to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

Be wary of anyone who contacts you afterwards promising to recover your money for a fee. The FTC warns about these [refund and recovery scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams).

## FAQ

### Is it legal to work as a tour guide in Colombia without a license?

Colombian rules require guides to hold a professional guide card and register in the RNT, and operating without RNT registration can lead to sanctions. This is general information, not legal advice; ask the guide or agency directly if you are unsure.

### Where do I find a guide's RNT number?

Ask for it. Registered agencies must include the RNT number in their advertising, so it often appears on websites, brochures and booking pages.

### Is it safe to pay a tour deposit by bank transfer?

A transfer is hard to reverse. Keep deposits small, pay a name that matches the booking, get written terms, and avoid anyone who insists on wire, gift card, crypto or payment-app payment only.

### What should I do if my guide seems different from the photos?

Don't leave with them. Call the agency on the number you already have, ask who was sent, and if you have a TRU ID, compare the photo from your lookup.

### Can VeraID check a guide who doesn't use the app?

No. VeraID only works when the guide has verified and has a TRU ID. For guides without one, use the RNT number, guide card, written terms and a traceable payment.
