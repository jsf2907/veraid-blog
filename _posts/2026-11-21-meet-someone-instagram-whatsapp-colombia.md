Meeting someone from Instagram or WhatsApp in Colombia can be perfectly safe if you treat the first meeting like any meeting with a stranger. Confirm they are a real person with a live video call, keep money out of the conversation, and meet in a busy public place with your own way home. These apps were built for messaging, not dating, so the safety checks are up to you.

## Why is meeting someone from Instagram or WhatsApp different from a dating app?

Dating apps add a few layers between you and a stranger: profiles tied to the app, optional photo verification badges, and report buttons built around dating. A DM on Instagram or a message on WhatsApp skips most of that. Anyone can create an account, use any photo, and message you directly.

That doesn't make every message suspicious. Plenty of travelers meet friends, dates and hosts this way. It just means the job of checking that a person is real, and that their story adds up, falls on you.

WhatsApp also works differently from a dating app in one important way: your phone number is part of your account. WhatsApp's own help page says there is no way to hide your phone number when using WhatsApp ([WhatsApp privacy settings](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/android/23225461)). Think about that before you move a conversation from Instagram to WhatsApp.

## How common are romance scams that start on social media?

Common enough to take seriously. In April 2026 the FTC reported that people lost $2.1 billion to scams that started on social media in 2025, and that nearly 60% of people who reported losing money to a romance scam in 2025 said it started on a social media platform. Facebook was named most often, with WhatsApp and Instagram a distant second and third ([FTC, April 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/new-ftc-data-show-people-have-lost-billions-social-media-scams)).

The FTC's consumer guide describes how these scams usually look: the person says it's love but lives far away, can't meet in person, and eventually asks for money for a ticket to visit, a visa, a medical bill or a fee, or offers to help you start investing in cryptocurrency ([FTC, what to know about romance scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)).

## How do I know if someone on Instagram or WhatsApp is real?

Use a few checks before you agree to meet:

- **Do a reverse image search.** The FTC suggests a reverse image search of the person's profile picture. If the photos show up under other names, stop there.
- **Look at the account history.** A real Instagram account usually has posts spread over time, tagged photos from friends and comments from people who know them. A new account with a handful of polished photos is worth a closer look.
- **Check WhatsApp's first-message signals.** When someone who isn't in your contacts messages you, WhatsApp shows whether they are in your contacts, whether you share any groups, and whether the number is from another country ([WhatsApp, how to stay safe](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/general/21197244)). A "local" who writes from a foreign number deserves a question.
- **Look for consistency.** Their name, city, job and story should line up across chats, photos and calls.
- **Ask for a live video call.** This is the single most useful check, and it gets its own section below.

## Why should I video call before meeting?

A live video call shows you that the person matches their photos, that they are where they say they are, and that they are willing to be seen. Someone who always has a broken camera, a bad connection or a reason to cancel the call is giving you an answer.

Keep the call simple. Ask them to say your name or wave, chat for a few minutes, and notice whether the face, voice and background fit what they told you. You don't need to interrogate anyone. A short, friendly call is normal before meeting someone you only know online.

If they refuse every call but are keen to meet in a private place, or quickly start talking about money, walk away.

## What are the red flags before a first meeting?

Most warning signs show up in the chat long before any meeting. Watch for:

- **Money in any form.** A request for help with a bill, a ticket, a visa, a fee or an "investment". The FTC's bottom line is to never send money or gifts to someone you haven't met in person.
- **Payment in hard-to-trace ways.** Gift card PINs, wire transfers, payment apps or crypto.
- **Pressure and speed.** Declaring strong feelings within days, or pushing to meet tonight.
- **A private first location.** Their apartment, your rental, or a place far from where you're staying.
- **Moving you off the platform fast.** Pushing to switch from Instagram to WhatsApp or another app before you know them.
- **A story that keeps changing.** Different names, ages or cities in different chats.
- **Can't meet, always an excuse.** The FTC notes that scammers often say they can't meet in person.

A few more habits help. Meet in a café, a busy plaza or a mall rather than a home or a quiet bar. Don't share the address where you're staying until you know the person well. WhatsApp itself advises sharing your location only with people you trust, so skip live location with someone new.

Dating meetups are a real concern in Colombia. A U.S. Embassy security alert reported eight suspicious deaths of U.S. citizens in Medellín over two months and warned travelers about the risks of meeting people through dating apps ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/South-America-Travel/US-warns-Americans-dating-app-danger-Colombia)). The same precautions apply to people you meet on Instagram or WhatsApp. For the latest official guidance, check the [U.S. State Department travel advisory for Colombia](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/colombia.html) for the current level before your trip.

## Which Instagram and WhatsApp settings help?

Both apps have tools that are worth setting up before you start chatting with strangers.

**On WhatsApp** ([privacy settings](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/android/23225461)):

- Set who can see your last seen and online status, profile photo, about and status updates to "My contacts" or "Nobody".
- Limit who can add you to groups.
- Turn on "Silence unknown callers" so calls from numbers you haven't saved don't ring.
- Turn off live location sharing when you don't need it.

**On Instagram** ([Instagram's guide to block, restrict and report](https://about.instagram.com/blog/tips-and-tricks/restrict-mute-block-report-guide/)):

- **Restrict** limits someone quietly, without alerting them: they won't see when you're online, and they can't tag or mention you.
- **Block** stops them from finding your profile, seeing your content or messaging you, and Instagram says people aren't notified when you block them.
- **Report** is anonymous, so the person won't know you reported them.

## What should I do if something goes wrong?

If you feel unsafe, leave and get to a public place. In an emergency in Colombia, call 123.

If it's a scam or harassment:

- **Stop replying and keep evidence.** Take screenshots of the profile, chats and any payment details.
- **Block and report.** On WhatsApp, blocking stops their messages and calls, and a report sends WhatsApp the last five messages they sent you without notifying them ([WhatsApp, block and report](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/iphone/20950271)). On Instagram, reporting is anonymous.
- **Call your bank** right away if you sent money.
- **Report to the police.** In Colombia you can report online through the Policía Nacional's ADenunciar service ([Policía Nacional](https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/una-herramienta-al-servicio-ciudadania-adenunciar)).
- **US travelers** can also report to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

Be wary of anyone who contacts you later offering to recover your money for a fee. The FTC warns about these [refund and recovery scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/refund-and-recovery-scams).

## FAQ

### Is it safe to meet someone from Instagram in Colombia?

It can be, if you confirm they are real with a video call, meet in a busy public place in daytime, use your own transport and tell a friend your plans. Never send money to someone you haven't met in person.

### Should I give my WhatsApp number to someone I met on Instagram?

Only when you're comfortable with them having it. WhatsApp shows your phone number to the people you message and there's no way to hide it, so keep chatting on Instagram until you've done a video call.

### What if they won't do a video call?

Treat it as a red flag. Repeated excuses to avoid a live call, combined with pressure to meet privately or talk about money, are reasons to stop.

### What is the emergency number in Colombia?

123 is Colombia's emergency number for police, ambulance and fire.

### Can VeraID verify someone I met on Instagram?
