To hire a private driver in Colombia safely, book through a hotel, a registered agency or a trusted recommendation, get the driver's name, photo, car and plate in writing, and pay in a way you can trace. At pickup, check that the person and the car match what you were sent before you get in. Most problems happen when a traveler gets into a car with someone whose identity was never confirmed.

## Why hire a private driver in Colombia at all?

Private drivers are popular for airport transfers, day trips out of Medellín, Bogotá or Cartagena, and long days with several stops. A good driver saves time, knows the roads and waits while you visit. It can also be a safer choice than picking up a car on the street.

Official travel advice is clear on that last point. The U.S. State Department's country page for Colombia says "Do not hail taxis on the street", notes that U.S. government employees are not allowed to hail taxis on the street because of the risk of assault or robbery, and advises using "a dispatch service or cell phone app whenever possible" ([U.S. State Department, Colombia country information](https://travel.state.gov/content/passports/en/country/colombia.html)). Canada's travel advice gives similar guidance: avoid hailing taxis on the street, use reputable companies through hotels or a ride-sharing app, and arrange airport pickup in advance with your travel agency or hotel ([Government of Canada, Colombia travel advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/colombia)).

A pre-booked private driver fits that advice, but only if you know who is actually driving.

## Where should I find a private driver in Colombia?

The safest bookings leave a record. Good options include:

- **Your hotel or host.** Many hotels work with drivers they use often and can tell you the driver's name and car.
- **A registered travel agency.** If an agency arranges the transfer, the booking, payment and complaints go through a business with a name and address.
- **A well-known booking platform** that has its own payment, cancellation and refund rules.
- **A recommendation from someone you trust**, who has used the same driver and can share their full name and number.

Be more careful with drivers who find you first: unsolicited messages, comments in travel groups, or people offering rides at the airport exit. Many independent drivers are honest, but these channels make it easy to copy someone else's name, photos and reviews. The FTC's travel scam advice is to search a company's name with words like "scam", "review" or "complaint" before you book, and to get recommendations from trusted sources ([FTC, avoid scams when you travel](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoid-scams-when-you-travel)).

## What should I ask before booking a private driver?

Ask the same questions every time, and get the answers in writing (email or chat you can screenshot):

- **Who is driving?** The driver's full name and a recent photo.
- **Which car?** Make, model, color and license plate.
- **What is the plan?** Pickup point and time, route or stops, waiting time, and drop-off.
- **What does it cost?** The total price, what it includes (tolls, parking, waiting) and the currency.
- **What if plans change?** The cancellation and refund policy, and what happens if your flight is late.
- **Who do I call?** A contact number for the agency or hotel, not only the driver's personal number.

A driver or agency that answers clearly is a good sign. Vague answers, a refusal to share the plate or name, or "just message me when you land" are reasons to keep looking.

## How should I pay a private driver?

Pay in a way you can trace. A card payment to a registered business or a booking platform with refund rules gives you a record if something goes wrong. Card dispute rights depend on your bank and come with deadlines, so keep receipts and messages.

The FTC warns that if someone insists you can only pay by wire transfer, gift card or cryptocurrency, it's "a sure sign of a scam", and says to be just as careful with demands to pay only through a payment app ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoid-scams-when-you-travel)). If a deposit is needed, keep it small, make sure the name on the payment matches the booking, and pay the rest in person after the ride. Don't change payment details because of a last-minute message, even if it seems to come from your driver.

## How do I get a safe ride from the airport in Colombia?

Decide how you'll leave the airport before you land. You have two good options: a driver you booked in advance, or the airport's official taxi service.

At Bogotá's El Dorado airport, the airport's own taxi page says pickup points are outside the terminal in lane 1, that staff who assign vehicles wear a uniform or distinctive clothing, and that Taxi Imperial is the authorized company. The site also has a tool where you enter a plate to check whether it is an official vehicle ([El Dorado airport, taxis](https://eldorado.aero/en/servicios/taxis); [plate check](https://eldorado.aero/en/servicios/taxis/verify-plate)). Canada's travel advice also notes that El Dorado allows only authorized taxis to pick up passengers at its terminals ([Government of Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/colombia)).

If you booked a private driver:

- Share your flight number so they can track delays.
- Agree on an exact meeting point and a sign with your name or a code word.
- Ignore anyone who approaches you inside the terminal offering a ride, even if they seem to know your name. Names can be read off a sign.
- Walk with the driver only after you've matched their face and name to the booking.

Two simple rules from Canada's advice also apply to any car: avoid vehicles without license plates, and never get into a car that already has another passenger in it ([Government of Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/colombia)).

## What if a different driver or car shows up?

Plans change for honest reasons: a driver gets sick, a car breaks down. The safe habit is that the hotel or agency tells you in advance, with the new driver's name, photo and plate. If someone unexpected is waiting and can't explain who sent them, don't get in. Call the hotel or agency on the number you already have, not a number the person at the curb gives you.

Canada's travel advice warns that express kidnappings, where victims are taken from the street or a taxi and forced to withdraw money from ATMs, happen in Colombia, often in affluent and tourist areas, and that they often occur in unlicensed taxis ([Government of Canada](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/colombia)). Confirming the driver before you get in is the single best way to avoid ending up in the wrong car. If you are ever threatened, the same advice is to stay calm and not resist.

For the latest official safety information, check the [U.S. State Department travel advisory for Colombia](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/colombia.html) for the current level before your trip.

## What else keeps me safe during the ride?

- **Share your trip.** Send a friend the driver's name, plate, pickup time and destination, and turn on live location sharing.
- **Keep valuables out of sight.** The U.S. State Department notes that crimes against tourists and people who look wealthy are common and well coordinated in cities, and that victims are often picked out by visible jewelry or expensive watches ([U.S. State Department](https://travel.state.gov/content/passports/en/country/colombia.html)).
- **Don't accept food or drinks from strangers.** The same page warns that scopolamine, a fast-acting drug, is sometimes slipped into food and drinks.
- **Trust your gut.** If the route makes no sense or the driver changes the plan, ask to stop somewhere busy and call your hotel.
- **Know the number.** In an emergency in Colombia, call 123.

If something goes wrong, keep screenshots of the booking, chats and payment. You can file a police report online through the Policía Nacional's ADenunciar service ([Policía Nacional](https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/una-herramienta-al-servicio-ciudadania-adenunciar)), and US travelers can also report scams to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

## FAQ

### Is it safe to use a private driver in Colombia?

It can be a good choice when the driver is booked in advance through a source you can trace and you confirm the person and the car at pickup. Official advice for Colombia recommends dispatch services, apps or pre-arranged pickups over hailing taxis on the street.

### Should I take a ride from someone offering at the airport exit?

No. Use a driver you booked in advance or the airport's official taxi service. At Bogotá's El Dorado, the airport lists its authorized taxi company and pickup area on its website.

### What details should my driver send me before pickup?

Their full name, a photo, the car's make, color and license plate, the exact meeting point and a contact number for the hotel or agency.

### Is it OK to pay a driver's deposit by bank transfer?

A transfer is hard to reverse. Keep any deposit small, pay a name that matches the booking, get written terms, and avoid anyone who insists on wire, gift card, crypto or payment-app payment only.

### Can VeraID check a driver who doesn't use the app?

No. VeraID only works when the driver has verified and has a TRU ID. For drivers without one, book through a hotel or agency, get the name and plate in writing, and pay in a way you can trace.
