- A short, live video call is the simplest way to check that an online match is the person in their photos before you meet.

Before a first date with someone you met online in Colombia, ask for a short live video call. Five to ten minutes is enough to see that the face matches the photos, that the person can talk naturally and that their story holds together. If they refuse, keep delaying or turn the conversation to money, you've learned something important without leaving home.

## Why video call a match before a first date?

Photos and chat can be copied, edited or written by someone else. A live conversation is much harder to fake for a whole call, and it gives you a feel for the person that text never will.

It also filters out the most common romance scam pattern. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission says plainly that "scammers say they can't meet you in person," often claiming to work abroad, on an oil rig, in the military or for an international organization ([FTC, what to know about romance scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)). Someone who also can't manage a two-minute video call is giving you a clear answer.

There is a safety reason too. Official travel advice for Colombia warns about online dating being used to target visitors. Canada's advice mentions an increase in deaths related to online dating applications in Bogotá, Cartagena and Medellín, and tells travelers to avoid isolated meetings and to tell a friend who they're meeting and where ([Government of Canada, Colombia travel advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/colombia)). The U.S. Embassy has issued a similar alert about dating apps ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/South-America-Travel/US-warns-Americans-dating-app-danger-Colombia)). A video call is one small step in a larger plan, not the whole plan.

## How do I ask a match for a video call without making it awkward?

Make it normal, light and about both of you. Most genuine people are happy to do it, and many expect it. A few ways to ask:

- "Before we meet, want to do a quick video call this evening? Ten minutes, just to say hi."
- "I always do a short video chat before a first date. When works for you?"
- "Easier to plan the date if we talk for a minute. Free for a quick call tomorrow?"

Keep it short and give a time window. A clear, friendly request is easy to say yes to, and a vague "maybe sometime" is easy to dodge. If they agree and suggest a time, that's already a good sign.

## Which app should I use for the call?

Start with the dating app's own video call if it has one. That way you can talk without giving out your phone number or other accounts. Bumble, for example, lets matched users start a video or voice call from the chat, and its help center notes "there's no need to exchange phone numbers" ([Bumble, in-app voice and video calls](https://support.bumble.com/hc/en-us/articles/32668295171613-In-app-voice-and-video-calls)).

If the app has no video calls, you can move to a messaging app once you're comfortable. Remember that a phone number is a lot of personal information to hand to a stranger, and on WhatsApp your number is visible to anyone you chat with. Review your privacy settings before you share it.

A few setup tips:

- **Choose your background.** A plain wall works. Avoid showing your building, street, hotel name or room number.
- **Use good light.** Face a window or a lamp so they can see you, and so you can see them.
- **Pick a time that suits you.** Not when you're rushing to the airport or after a long night out.
- **Keep it short.** Ten minutes is plenty. You can always talk longer next time.

## What should I look for during the video call?

You're not interviewing them. You're checking a few simple things while having a normal conversation.

- **Do they match their photos?** Same face, roughly the same age, same general look. Lighting and angles change things, but the person should be recognizable.
- **Is it live?** Natural reactions, eye contact, laughter at the right moments. Ask something light that a person in a pre-recorded clip couldn't answer, such as "what's that behind you?"
- **Does the story hold?** Their job, city and plans should sound like what they've told you in chat.
- **How do they treat a simple question?** Friendly answers are a good sign. Pushing you to share your hotel, your plans or your money is not.

AI tools now make fake images, voices and video easier to produce. The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report counted almost $893 million in reported losses from AI-related scams, driven partly by voice cloning and deepfake images and videos ([Malwarebytes, June 2026](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/06/americans-lost-nearly-900-million-to-ai-powered-scams-fbi-says)). In a separate warning about fake video chats, the FBI suggests looking for "subtle imperfections in images and videos" and lag in voice calls ([FBI IC3 public service announcement, July 2026](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260720)). Odd blurring around the face, a frozen expression, or a picture that won't turn when asked are reasons to slow down.

## What are the red flags before or during the call?

These patterns come up again and again in romance scam warnings. One on its own may have an innocent explanation; several together are a strong reason to stop.

- **Endless excuses.** The camera is broken, the connection is bad, they're at work, they'll call "tomorrow" every day.
- **Camera always off, or always dark.** You hear a voice but never really see a face.
- **A rushed romance.** Big feelings within days, and pressure to move the chat off the app.
- **Money, any form.** A medical bill, a ticket, a visa, an investment tip. The FTC's advice is simple: "Never send money or gifts to a sweetheart you haven't met in person" ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams)).
- **Odd payment methods.** Wire transfers, gift cards, payment apps or crypto. The FTC lists these as the ways scammers tell people to pay.
- **Pressure about the first meeting.** They insist on a private place, want to pick you up, or ask which hotel or apartment you're in.

If something feels wrong, you can end the call politely. You don't owe anyone a second chance to explain.

## The call went well. How do I plan the first date?

A good call is a good sign, not a guarantee. Keep the rest of the plan just as careful:

1. **Meet in a busy public place** in daylight or early evening, such as a café or a restaurant in a central area.
2. **Get there and leave on your own.** Use a ride from an app or a dispatch service rather than hailing a taxi on the street, and don't accept a lift from your date the first time ([U.S. State Department, Colombia country information](https://travel.state.gov/content/passports/en/country/colombia.html)).
3. **Tell a friend** who you're meeting, where and when, and share your live location if you like.
4. **Watch your drink and your food.** Don't leave them unattended, and don't accept anything opened by someone else.
5. **Don't bring a new date back to your accommodation**, and don't go to theirs on a first meeting. Canada's travel advice says the same.
6. **Keep your phone charged** and know that 123 is Colombia's emergency number.

It's also worth checking the latest U.S. travel advisory for Colombia before you travel, since its level and details can change ([U.S. State Department, Colombia travel advisory](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/colombia.html)).

## What if a match turns out to be fake or asks for money?

Stop replying, don't send anything, and don't argue. Then:

- **Report the profile** on the dating app or site where you met.
- **Report the scam** to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) if you're in the U.S., or to the fraud authority in your home country.
- **In Colombia,** you can file a police report online through the Policía Nacional's ADenunciar service ([Policía Nacional](https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/una-herramienta-al-servicio-ciudadania-adenunciar)). In an emergency, call 123.
- **Tell someone you trust.** It's easier to see a scam clearly when you talk it through.

This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ

### How long should a video call before a first date be?

Five to ten minutes is enough. The goal is to see that the person matches their photos and can talk naturally, not to have the whole date on camera.

### Is it rude to ask a match for a video call?

No. It's a common, sensible step, especially when you're traveling. A friendly, specific request ("quick call tonight around 7?") is easy for a genuine person to accept.

### What if they say their camera doesn't work?

Offer another time or another app. If the excuses keep coming, treat it as a red flag. The FTC notes that romance scammers often have reasons they can't meet, and the same goes for not showing their face.

### Can a video call be faked?

AI tools can fake images, voices and some video, so a call isn't proof on its own. Ask a light, live question, look for glitches around the face, and still follow the public-meeting steps above.
