## Key takeaways

- Don't hail taxis on the street in Bogotá. Use the authorized taxi line at El Dorado airport, a dispatch service, an app, or a pickup arranged through your hotel.
- TransMilenio is the fastest way across the city, but stations get crowded. Keep your phone and wallet out of reach, and get a TuLlave card for the simplest payment.
- Explore La Candelaria and walk up Monserrate in daylight, and check the trail's opening hours before you go.
- Meet new people, including online matches, in busy public places, and don't bring someone you just met back to your room.
- If your guide, driver or host has a VeraID TRU ID, look it up before you meet to see their photo, first name and verification status.

Bogotá is a big, busy capital, and most visits go smoothly when you plan how you will move around and who you will meet. The short version: use authorized taxis or a pickup you booked, keep your phone out of sight in crowds, see the old town and Monserrate by day, and confirm who a guide, driver or date really is before you meet them. Here is how to do each of those well.

## Is Bogotá safe for tourists right now?

Many trips to Bogotá go without problems, but petty theft happens in crowded areas and travel advisories for Colombia are serious. The U.S. State Department rates countries by level, and that level can change, so check the current one on the [U.S. State Department's Colombia travel advisory](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/colombia.html) before your trip.

The State Department's country page for Colombia gives practical warnings that apply in Bogotá: don't hail taxis on the street, use a dispatch service or app when you can, watch for scopolamine in food and drinks, and avoid showing jewelry or expensive watches, because criminals pick out tourists who look wealthy ([U.S. State Department, Colombia country information](https://travel.state.gov/content/passports/en/country/colombia.html)).

None of this means you should stay in your hotel. It means you should plan your rides, keep valuables out of sight, and be careful with strangers who move fast.

## How do I get from El Dorado airport to my hotel safely?

You have three good options:

- **A pickup you booked.** Your hotel, host or a registered agency sends a driver whose name, photo, car and plate you have in writing.
- **The authorized taxi line.** El Dorado's taxi service has a pickup area in lane 1 just outside the terminal, with uniformed staff who assign taxis. The airport also has an online tool to check a taxi's plate ([El Dorado Airport, taxis](https://eldorado.aero/en/servicios/taxis) and [plate check](https://eldorado.aero/en/servicios/taxis/verify-plate)).
- **A ride-hailing or taxi app**, so the trip and the driver's details are recorded.

What to avoid: people who walk up to you in the arrivals hall offering a ride. Even when they are friendly, you don't know who they are, and that is exactly the gap you want to close.

If you are on a budget, the city's TransMilenio system also serves the airport. A 2022 city guide for first-time users notes a TuLlave card point inside El Dorado ([Bogotá city government, TransMilenio tips](https://bogota.gov.co/en/international/tips-using-transmilenio-system-your-first-visit-bogota)). With big bags or a late arrival, a booked car or the taxi line is usually easier.

## Are taxis and ride apps safe in Bogotá?

Taxis and apps are part of daily life in Bogotá, and the main rule is simple: don't get into a random taxi on the street. Travel safety guides warn that fake taxis are used for robberies and so-called "express kidnappings", usually late at night ([World Nomads, Bogotá safety](https://www.worldnomads.com/travel-safety/south-america/colombia/beware-the-dangers-of-bogota)).

Safer habits:

- Order a taxi through an app or have your hotel, restaurant or bar call one for you.
- Before you get in, check that the plate and car match what the app or dispatcher told you.
- Share your trip with a friend or travel partner.
- Sit in the back and keep your phone low, not on your lap by the window.
- If the driver, car or plate doesn't match, don't get in. Cancel and order another.

For a full day with a private driver, see our guide to [hiring a private driver in Colombia](/private-driver-colombia/).

## How do I use TransMilenio without getting pickpocketed?

TransMilenio is Bogotá's bus rapid transit system, and it is fast and cheap. The weak point is crowding: travel guides note that stations are known for pickpockets ([World Nomads, Bogotá safety](https://www.worldnomads.com/travel-safety/south-america/colombia/beware-the-dangers-of-bogota)).

How to ride smart:

- **Pay the easy way.** The TuLlave card is the standard fare card. In January 2026 TransMilenio started accepting Visa and Mastercard cards and phone payments at some stations, but those count as a full fare each time, while transfers and discounts only work with TuLlave. The fare was COP 3,550 from January 14, 2026 ([Publimetro, January 2026](https://www.publimetro.co/bogota/2026/01/20/ya-no-necesitara-solo-la-tarjeta-tullave-para-el-pasaje-transmilenio-habilito-el-pago-con-celulares-y-tarjetas-bancarias/)).
- **Keep things zipped.** Wear your backpack on your front in crowds, and keep your phone in a front pocket or zipped bag.
- **Skip peak hours if you can.** Less crowding means fewer chances for theft.
- **Plan the route before you leave.** The city recommends the official TransMi app for routes ([Bogotá city government](https://bogota.gov.co/en/international/tips-using-transmilenio-system-your-first-visit-bogota)), so you are not standing on a platform studying a map.
- **Take a taxi late at night.** After dark, an app or dispatched taxi to your door is the safer choice.

## Is La Candelaria safe to visit?

La Candelaria is Bogotá's oldest neighborhood and the center of most first visits, with museums, churches and colonial streets around Plaza de Bolívar. Fodor's describes most of the best museums as within a five-block radius of the plaza and walkable by day "with usual care being taken", but says the area should be avoided at night ([Fodor's, La Candelaria](https://www.fodors.com/world/south-america/colombia/bogota/neighborhoods/la-candelaria)). Travel guides also mention pickpocketing in busy spots by day and robberies at night ([World Nomads](https://www.worldnomads.com/travel-safety/south-america/colombia/beware-the-dangers-of-bogota)).

A good plan: explore La Candelaria in the morning and afternoon, go with a registered guide if you want context and company, and take a taxi or app ride back to your hotel before it gets dark. Withdraw cash inside a bank or mall rather than at street ATMs, since thieves are known to watch cash machines.

## How do I visit Monserrate safely?

Monserrate, the mountain above the city center, is one of Bogotá's best views. You can ride the funicular or cable car, or walk up the trail.

- **Check the hours first.** The official Monserrate site lists the walking trail as open for the climb from 5 a.m. to 1 p.m. and for the descent until 4 p.m., closed on Tuesdays for maintenance, with hours that can change at the discretion of the city's sports and recreation institute (IDRD) ([Monserrate, schedules and prices](https://monserrate.co/en/schedules-and-prices)).
- **Go early and with others.** The trail only opens for the climb in the morning, and the city has posted uniformed officers on the trail from 5 a.m. to protect early climbers ([Bogotá city government, 2022](https://bogota.gov.co/mi-ciudad/seguridad/se-ampliara-cobertura-de-horario-de-uniformados-en-sendero-monserrate)).
- **Take it slow.** Bogotá is high up and the trail is steep. If you are not used to altitude, the funicular or cable car is an easy alternative.
- **Plan the way down.** Don't walk down the trail or the surrounding streets late in the day; ride down and take a taxi from the base.

## How do I meet guides, drivers and new people safely in Bogotá?

Many of the best moments in Bogotá come from people: a guide who knows the history, a driver who handles a day trip, a host who gives you local tips, or someone you met on an app. The goal is to confirm who they are before you are alone with them.

Official advice is direct about dating apps. Canada's travel advice has reported an increase in deaths related to online dating apps in cities including Bogotá, and recommends avoiding isolated meetings, not bringing new acquaintances to your accommodation, and telling a friend who you are meeting and where ([Government of Canada, Colombia travel advice](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/colombia)). The U.S. Embassy has also warned about U.S. citizens harmed after meeting people through dating apps in Colombia ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/South-America-Travel/US-warns-Americans-dating-app-danger-Colombia)).

Practical steps for anyone new:

- **Do a live video call first** with an online match. It is a good sign, not a guarantee. See our guide to [video calls before a first date](/video-call-before-first-date-colombia/).
- **Meet in a busy public place** such as a café or restaurant in an area you know, and get there on your own.
- **Watch your drink and food**, and don't accept snacks, drinks or cigarettes from people you just met.
- **Tell a friend** the person's name, profile and where you are going, and share your location.
- **Leave if something feels off.** You don't owe anyone an explanation.

For guides and drivers, ask for their full name, photo and booking details in writing, and pay in a way you can trace. Our guide to [booking a tour guide in Colombia](/book-tour-guide-colombia-safely/) covers what to check.

## How does VeraID help in Bogotá?

[VeraID](https://veraid.org/) helps you check who you are meeting before you meet them. Guides, drivers, hosts and other professionals verify their government ID with a biometric check plus a human review, and receive a permanent TRU ID. As a visitor, you look up that TRU ID in the app and see their photo, first name and verification status, so you can confirm the person who shows up is the person you booked.

Visitors never verify their own ID. You simply choose a visitor plan to look people up: $5.99, $14.99 or $19.99 USD.

To be clear about the limits: VeraID only works if the person you are meeting has a TRU ID. It has no panic button, check-in or monitoring feature, so keep using the everyday habits above and call 123 if you are ever in danger. Most online matches won't have a TRU ID, so for dating, a live video call and the public-meeting steps are your best tools.

## What should I do if something goes wrong in Bogotá?

If you are in danger, call **123**, Colombia's emergency number. If you are robbed, hand over your belongings; your safety matters more than your phone. Afterward:

- File a police report. Colombia's National Police offers an online reporting tool called ADenunciar ([Policía Nacional](https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/una-herramienta-al-servicio-ciudadania-adenunciar)).
- Contact your bank to block cards, and your embassy if your passport was taken. U.S. citizens can find embassy contacts on the [State Department's Colombia page](https://travel.state.gov/content/passports/en/country/colombia.html).
- If you think you were drugged, get medical help as soon as possible.

This is general information, not legal or medical advice.

## FAQ

**Is it safe to take a taxi from El Dorado airport?**
Yes, if you use the authorized taxi line in lane 1 outside the terminal, a ride app, or a pickup you booked. Don't accept rides from people who approach you inside.

**Can I pay for TransMilenio with my bank card?**
Some stations started accepting Visa and Mastercard cards and phone payments in January 2026, charged as a full fare each time. A TuLlave card is still the simplest option and the only one with free transfers.

**Is La Candelaria safe at night?**
It is best visited by day. Guides advise avoiding the area at night, so plan to head back by taxi or app before dark.

**Can I walk up Monserrate?**
Yes, during the trail's opening hours, which the official site lists as 5 a.m. to 1 p.m. for the climb, closed on Tuesdays. Go in the morning with others and check the hours before you go.

**Can I use VeraID to check a dating match in Bogotá?**
Only if they have a TRU ID, which most matches won't. For dates, do a live video call, meet in a busy public place, and tell a friend where you are going.
